In March, Shiyo 729, a certain visitor arrived.
The visitor was riding in a fine automobile.
With someone like that suddenly appearing, the residents began to stir as well.
They were residents wary of outsiders at the best of times.
I watched the scene from the window of the house, and the visitor seemed to be heading our way.
"Pardon the intrusion!"
The visitor opened the door of the Niimura house and shouted those words.
"May I ask who you are?"
Now 65 years old, I feigned calm and received the visitor.
This visitor's back was ramrod straight, the expression firm and set.
In the mid-thirties, I would guess.
After a salute, the stance quietly eased.
It seemed to be the utmost show of courtesy.
"I have been dispatched by the government, and my name is Hajime Shinozaki! 　I have a matter to discuss with the representative of this settlement, and so I have come from Tokyo!"
The government...? What business could such a visitor possibly have?
I was somewhat wary, but if this person had come all the way from Tokyo alone, I could hardly send such a visitor away out of hand.
"Please, come in."
Beyond the open door―from the hedge of the Niimura house, the residents stared suspiciously at Shinozaki-san.
If Shinozaki-san tried anything strange―
That situation, too, played its part in my deciding to invite him in.
I offered him tea and sweets.
"Ah, I am much obliged."
Shinozaki-san savored the tea and sweets without reserve.
Even by car, anyone who had come so far under this cold sky would surely be hungry.
"I am Kikyo Niimura, the representative of this settlement. What might your business be?"
I waited until Shinozaki-san had drained his tea before raising the matter.
At that, Shinozaki-san took a sheet of paper from his bag.
It appeared to be an old letter.
Shinozaki-san silently unfolded the letter and held it out before me.
I have come from Arata, a remote region.
Those were its opening words.
And at the end, the signature 'Arata Settlement Representative　Ko Niimura.'
Ko-san's letter!?
The day I first met Ko-san, our talk by the river, the wedding ceremony, and― his death.
All of it came rushing back to me at once.
For a while, I gazed at the letter.
"I have come in the belief that now, of all times, this drug may be of service to the country."
Shinozaki-san's voice brought me back to myself.
At the same time, I remembered my purpose in marrying into this place.
Of course, first and foremost had been my wish to be with Ko-san, someone irreplaceable to me.
But the other was to uncover the true nature of Droga and the curse-killing.
Only now did it occur to me.
Why had Ko-san and Mother killed themselves?
Had it truly been suicide?
Or else...
I, too, had come to feel my age.
I had forgotten that purpose and put everyone's safety first.
But as long as this settlement lived alongside Droga, was that safety not a sham?
Such thoughts had crossed my mind now and then.
But having reached this age, I had begun to question whether things could truly stay as they were.
"Shinozaki-san, in other words, you want the cherry trees here?"
"Your quick grasp of things is a great help. I would ask for your cooperation."
"You came all the way from Tokyo for that?"
"............ It is as you say."
"Have you ever been to Iizawa Prefecture before?"
"............ No, this is my first time."
At that moment, I saw through Shinozaki-san's lie.
This person had not come from Tokyo.
For his accent was the way people speak in Iizawa Prefecture.
Having come from Tokyo myself, I could tell at once.
―A long silence.
"Is this drug still in use now?"
"No, that should no longer be so."
"I see..."
I lied as well.
Droga was still in use, even now.
But I could not see what this person was after.
What reason could someone from Iizawa Prefecture have for wanting the cherry trees badly enough to lie about being from the government?
"What do you say? The cherry trees here..."
"How many do you need?"
"If it is possible, we would like as many as you can give."
"How would you carry so many cherry trees? 　Surely you do not intend to make everyone in Arata carry them?"
"C-certainly not! We have no such intention whatsoever! 　It depends on how many you can provide, but we are also considering building a railway."
"A railway?"
"Yes. Whether it is one tree or a thousand, we will do everything ourselves, from the felling to the hauling."
It was a strange thing.
Surely no one but the state itself could raise the funds to build a railway.
"Of course, you will be analyzing Droga scientifically, will you not?"
"Yes. Without fail."
"............ You may take as many as you like."
"I-is that true!?"
"However, there are conditions."
"What might they be...?"
The conditions I set―
・The cherry tree of the Niimura main house alone is to be left standing
・The results of the research are to be disclosed to this settlement in writing
Those two.
I could not quite bring myself to part with every last cherry tree.
In the end, who this man really was remained unknown, but if Droga was to be analyzed scientifically, the truth I had wanted to know― and a way to go on truly protecting this settlement would surely become clear.
