Ko-sama had put the Noh mask on again.
Naturally, this strange getup drew the attention of the people around us.
But that was fine.
This way, he would pass as nothing more than a man in odd clothes.
Westerners were still rare here in Tokyo, so I had suggested that he wear the Noh mask.
Paying no mind to the eyes around us, Ko-sama and I walked side by side and told each other where we came from.
Why were we, meeting for the first time, able to talk without much wariness?
There were two reasons.
One was that I was very interested in Western culture.
Ko-sama, though Japanese, had a Westerner among his ancestors, and in his homeland, a place called Arata, Western culture still remained even now.
And the other was Ko-sama's character.
Ko-sama had apparently never left Arata before, and he said he did not know how he ought to deal with people from outside.
So he was having trouble judging how much of his own background he could reveal.
Arata, it seemed, had always been closed to outsiders by nature, but since coming here to Tokyo, Ko-sama had come into contact with its advanced culture and was very excited about it.
Ko-sama was 16, the same as me.
He had come here to Tokyo as Arata's representative, it seemed.
Ko-sama had come from Arata to Tokyo, so he said, to present a certain thing to the Emperor.
But, understandably, he would not tell me what it was he was presenting.
It seemed he had been strictly warned never to speak of this one thing except at the presentation itself.
"I see. So Kikyo-dono, you're eagerly studying Western culture so as not to fall behind the Great Powers."
"Yes. Father is always scolding me, telling me a woman mustn't act so forward."
"A woman acting forward...? 　Is that a bad thing?"
"Huh?"
"No, it's just that in my homeland, women govern the whole settlement. It isn't 'because you're a man' or 'because you're a woman': the natural order is that the capable lead everyone. And everyone supports the capable ones. One for all, and all for one."
To think there was a place with such an ideal culture.
Ko-sama called the culture of Tokyo advanced.
But to me, it was Arata's way of thinking, its spirit of helping one another, that was truly advanced.
"Well, I'll be off, then. Kikyo-dono, thank you for guiding me."
"Not at all... U-uh, how soon will you be back...?"
"Hmm, I couldn't say... What's the matter?"
"If you don't mind, Ko-sama, I was hoping we could talk a little longer."
But Ko-sama tilted his head slightly.
"I'm sorry, but once my business is done, I have to go straight back to Arata..."
"I see..."
"Well, I'll be off, then."
Ko-sama went on to the castle just like that.
Such a fine person, and I had thought I was lucky to meet him. What a shame...
