―Time passed after that, and I would come to learn more about this settlement, about Droga, and about the events of the past.
"Better not to use Droga?"
"Yes. I can't shake the feeling that that drug is some kind of trap..."
That was what I advised Ko-san, who had been minding the child in my place.
5 years had passed since I came to the Arata settlement, and Ko-san, too, had begun going out to hunt using Droga.
"A trap? What do you mean? 　It's only a drug made from the roots of cherry trees―"
"That's certainly so. But... from what I've found so far, it has been more than 400 years since Droga appeared. And during those 400 years, in this settlement, strange events..."
I had been investigating not only Droga but also the events that had taken place in this settlement until then.
In the past, it seemed, this settlement had come to the brink of destruction several times.
This settlement hides secrets that even its residents do not yet know.
Time is a heartless thing. There must have been some warning in those days, but it gradually becomes an empty formality and is lost from people's memory.
Even though there was a warning for escaping that crisis, as time passes it sinks into mere lore and myth.
Among all this, there was one thing that especially caught my attention.
It was the phenomenon known as 'Green-Eye Curse-Killing.'
This too was written in the storehouse books, of course. In this settlement, every so often, a child is born with green eyes.
In that book,
'A green-eyed boy shall be raised as he is. A green-eyed girl shall be killed at once.'
is written, or words to that effect.
The only child born since I came to this settlement was my child with Ko-san.
So I did not know whether a child connected with that 'Green-Eye Curse-Killing' had ever really been born.
But even if I wanted to confirm it, asking Ko-san, 'Do you kill green-eyed girls?' made me a little uneasy.
And I could not even tell whether it had anything to do with Droga.
And yet―
"Uh, Ko-san... Do you know of the Green-Eye Curse-Killing?"
"Huh?"
Ko-san, who had been sitting in a chair holding the child, stopped moving.
From this reaction of Ko-san's, I could tell how he had taken my words.
