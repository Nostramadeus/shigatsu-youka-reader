Townspeople, thank you for stopping to listen in this heat today.
My name is Choji Nakajima, and I am a candidate in next month's mayoral election.
I have already had the honor of serving as mayor for 5 terms, 20 years.
All this time, I have thought about how to make Motoki-cho grow, how to make all of you townspeople more prosperous, and that is where I have focused my efforts.
As a result, our population has grown about 8 times compared to 20 years ago, and our aging rate, at about 13%, has reached the lowest level in Japan.
Talented people gather in Motoki-cho from other cities, towns and villages, and from other prefectures, and it is a municipality achieving rapid growth in every respect: economic growth, better welfare, regional revitalization and more.
Nevertheless, I will not be content with that. Dreaming of making Motoki-cho the most livable place in Japan, setting that as my goal, and then making it a reality: that is what I intend to achieve in my next term.
But looking back, 2 months ago a tragic incident occurred here in Motoki-cho.
It was an incident shocking enough to be reported sensationally all over Japan, and it was the moment Motoki-cho's myth of safety and security utterly collapsed.
Until then, Motoki-cho had been a municipality with one of the lowest crime rates in the country.
Resting on that, we were lax in our approach to security― that is, to 'a safe life.' That is what I most need to reflect on.
When the population increases, the number of crimes inevitably increases.
When a major incident occurs, unease inevitably lingers among the residents.
If this problem is left alone, making this the best town in Japan will be utterly out of reach.
Here and now, I put forward three pledges.
One―I will develop Motoki-cho further and make it the municipality with the highest incomes in Japan.
By granting subsidies to companies that place their head offices in Motoki-cho and bringing about further regional revitalization, I will raise your incomes even further.
This will also make it possible to promote a policy under which major corporations place the head offices of their group companies in Motoki-cho, and to gather a part of Japan's economy here.
Two―I will further strengthen Motoki-cho's tourism policy.
As you know, Motoki-cho is blessed with wonderful cherry blossoms.
In recent years their economic effect has been tremendous, and they are now an essential resource Motoki-cho cannot do without.
Along with branding Motoki-cho's cherry blossoms, I aim to make use of this basin's terrain to build a town that many tourists will love in summer, fall and winter as well.
The funds for the subsidies in my first pledge will be drawn from this tourism revenue and put to use for Motoki-cho's further development.
Three―I will make Motoki-cho a safe and secure town.
As I said a moment ago, the myth of Motoki-cho's safety has collapsed.
There may also be townspeople who fear that population growth and expanded tourism will rob them of a safe life.
During my term, I will install 1,000 security cameras in the town and see to swift resolution of incidents and thorough crime deterrence.
In addition, to prevent the psychology that makes crime more likely in run-down places― the so-called broken-windows theory, I will set up a committee to mend the places where the town is fraying.
So once I am reelected mayor, you will no longer need to worry about living in fear of violent crime.
Working with the Sawa Prefectural Police, I will make Motoki-cho an even safer town than before.
Over the years, I have made many pledges.
I take pride in having fulfilled every one of them and contributed to Motoki-cho's development.
So the three pledges I just named― these, too, will be achieved in the next term, within 4 years.
Higher incomes for you, expanded tourism, and a safe and secure life.
If you reelect me as mayor, these will certainly be yours within 4 years.
Please, townspeople of Motoki-cho.
Give me the chance of 4 more years.
It is a chance for me, for all of you, and for Motoki-cho to grow great, like the goshinboku in Central Park.
Thank you for listening in this heat.
