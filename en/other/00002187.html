Droga.
The more I learn about it, the more mysterious a drug it is.
It is refined from the roots of the cherry trees that grow wild in the Arata settlement. The finished crystals are heated over a flame, and the smoke is inhaled.
Then, it is said, one gains the strength of demon warriors.
If one uses Droga when hunting, one can catch prey with the greatest ease.
Thanks to this, the Arata settlement never lacks for food, and everyone lives in plenty.
Truly a drug from heaven, a blessing bestowed by the gods and the Buddhas.
Or so an ordinary person would say.
However, causality exists in all things.
I have no wish to deny the faith that believes in the gods and the Buddhas, but a mysterious thing must not be taken as nothing more than a mysterious phenomenon.
In it there is always a reason, a cause, and an effect.
So Droga, too, is a drug born under some form of causality.
My work is to investigate that.
To do so, I must go through the records of the past.
Droga was invented here about 400 years ago, I am told, but no one knows the details.
Yet for a drug such as this to have gone unrecorded is unthinkable.
There must surely be records of Droga.
That said, what these storehouse books deal with are Western concepts such as shinigami and witches.
Though the settlement has had dealings with Westerners since long ago, that culture seems to have taken root remarkably deeply.
The Grand Witch, who governs the running of this settlement, and the several witches who assist her.
Even the word Droga apparently means 'drug' in the West.
A shinigami is a kind of youkai that tells the living when they will die and guides them to the other world.
In this settlement, however, the dead are called shinigami.
It is in the memorial service that many of these elements come together.
At a memorial service, everyone attending puts on a Noh mask and the red vestments, and they mourn the dead as shinigami.
Yet the service itself is very simple: the Grand Witch merely destroys the shinigami's garments in front of the mourners.
It ends with only that, and there are no incense sticks and no offering of incense.
If anything, it is a rite more like vanquishing a shinigami.
And another strange thing is that this settlement enshrines a mummy as an idol.
The mummy of a girl called Verde.
She has been revered, it seems, ever since she saved this settlement about 300 years ago.
I came to think that this odd memorial service, too, might have some meaning.
