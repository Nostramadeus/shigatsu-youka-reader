If an organism makes its own energy like a single-celled one (by photosynthesis and so on), grows fast, and breeds prolifically, then even if its environment changes drastically, a few may mutate and adapt. But one that, like a multicellular organism, breeds poorly and cannot make energy risks, without such adaptive evolution, the extinction of its species in a short time. And if the species grows too numerous, the outside energy it needs (food) will surely run out.\n　For the individual, having no lifespan is ideal, but for the species as a whole, it is better to hope for evolution through the next generation's mutations and to correct the competition for energy within the species. Death is therefore a function for one's descendants, and going against it hastens the extinction of the species. Indeed, the life that flourishes most visibly on Earth is the multicellular kind with lifespans.\n　Wishing for agelessness and immortality could, from one point of view, be called nothing more than the ego of the living.Immortal Organisms

Here harmful substances and large substances are kept from entering, and necessary enzymes and the like are exchanged. Normally nothing the size of a spore can get through, but Shigatsu Youka's spores work their way into the blood-vessel wall just before the barrier, rapidly undergo cell division there, and are released from the vessel wall on the brain side. In other words, they can reach the brain by passing through the inside of the vessel wall without going through the barrier. Also, the brain organs called the circumventricular organs (CVO) are among the most vital sites for sustaining life, but because they release secretions such as brain hormones into the body, they have no barrier and are called 'the windows of the brain.'
