Haruka and the others had invited me, but I had turned them down, and I was lying face down on the bed in my room.
How much time had passed with me lying like this?
Today was a half day with only the entrance ceremony, so I had summoned all my strength to avoid ending up in the nurse's room―
I had walked home from school on unsteady legs and finally made it back.
Had an hour gone by since I collapsed onto the bed, no, three hours?
It was almost time for the orange evening sun to dye the room.
Haruka and the others, what are they doing now?
Goto must be enjoying food-stall menu items like yakisoba and fried chicken.
I'm grateful they invited me to the festival, but for me, the Cherry Blossom Festival is suicide.
"Haah..."
It's not that I want to deny how everyone loves the cherry blossoms.
April has only just begun, and plenty of people must be excited about their new lives.
No, maybe a lot of people get depressed at this time of year as well.
Plenty of people must be stressed by the change in their surroundings.
But in my case, it's different.
The cherry blossoms― When the season comes and the cherry blossoms bloom, I―
Through the open window, along with a light breeze, came the bustle of the outside.
Now and then a child's voice, now and then the sound of a car, now and then the sound of the wind.

And then... The intercom.
Getting up was a chore― I thought about pretending nobody was home, but...
Okay, okay, I'm opening it now.
When I opened the door, a high-school girl with short hair was standing there.
Just as I thought, it was Haruka.
"Hey! How's it going?"
"Yeah... so-so."
To her cheerful greeting, the same as always, I answered with something like a polite smile.
The corners of my mouth wouldn't lift properly.
"Hmm... So your April sickness still isn't any better this year." [TN: A pun on gogatsu-byo, 'May sickness,' Japan's term for the May blues.]
"Well, it happens every year, so I'm used to it by now."
"I see... Over on our end, Goto went around stuffing her face at one food stall after another, and it was rough. Going 'Operation start!' again and all that. What does she think my wallet is..."
Even as she said that, Haruka sounded happy telling me.
"Ha! Goto may be tiny, but she's a big eater. Where does all that food go?"

"My stomach's been off since yesterday too, so I only snacked a little, though. So, here. Souvenir!"
I was handed a plastic bag, neither big nor small.
"It's takoyaki, so eat it while it's hot. See you!"
Soon after our door closed, the sound of the door next to ours opening and closing echoed faintly along the outside walkway.
Haruka had left in a hurry, but she was probably worried about the strain on my body.
Making Haruka worry about me like this has become my routine every year at this time.
"Haah..."
Heaving another big sigh, I took the plastic bag and went back to my room.
It had gotten chilly, so I closed the window.
I put the takoyaki on the desk and crawled into bed.
"Haruka... thank you..."
Sure enough, my best friend knows me well.
She came by like this last year, and the year before that, too, didn't she?
I'm always the one being looked after, and I've hardly done anything to thank her.
The day before yesterday Haruka seemed to have an upset stomach, but the next time Haruka isn't feeling well, I'll bring a gift along and go visit her.
Haruka calls this gloomy mood of mine April sickness.
In cherry blossom season, I sink into a funk, like someone with the May blues.
I don't really understand it, but it seems my constitution can't tolerate cherry blossoms.
For me, early April is nothing but pain.
Seeing Haruka's face, I felt a little better, I think.
And, maybe because the tension had eased, a pleasant drowsiness stroked my eyes.
I should sleep now, even a little, while I can. My mind and body must both be tired.

For my part, it's a little lonely.
"Dad's not here, huh. And I was going to eat takoyaki with him."

Dad and Mom both like Haruka very much.
Mom put the takoyaki in the microwave, and in the meantime moved the stir-fry from the frying pan onto plates.
I had no appetite, but takoyaki that Haruka had given me, I could probably manage somehow.
"Sorry to keep you waiting. Okay, let's eat."
Rice and miso soup, and then dried horse mackerel and kinpira gobo.
To this plain dinner table, the takoyaki was added.
"Oh..."
"Oh..."
On the surface of the takoyaki sat a single cherry blossom petal.
"Sorry, I'll take it off right away..."
Mom's face had clouded over.
But for me, not eating this is not an option.
Just because I hate cherry blossoms, to refuse Haruka's get-well present, of all things.
"I-I'm fine! Not eating it over something this small..."
I lifted the takoyaki with my chopsticks.
"Ngh... guhaah..."
I rushed into the bathroom and crouched there, hunched over the toilet bowl.
"Ngh... hurk..."
Nothing came up from my empty stomach.
And yet I was racked by violent nausea and dizziness.
"Natsumi, are you okay!?"
"Hack! Hack! I... I'm fine... This always happens... Go on and eat..."
"Natsumi... All right. Call me if anything happens."
"Yeah..."
Something like this is no big deal.
It happens all the time.
This is the April sickness that comes over me every year.
Strictly speaking, I suppose you might call it cherry-blossom phobia.
Ever since I was old enough to remember, I've gotten sick like this at this time of year.
So I'm used to it by now.
I'm used to the symptoms.
But the cherry blossoms themselves are the one thing I can never get used to.
"Haah... haah..."
I steadied my breathing and calmed myself down.
Maybe I'm a little better now.
I thought the nap had made me feel better, but give it a trigger and this is what happens.
Even if it was something Haruka gave me, maybe I pushed myself a little too hard...
"Are you okay?"
Mom was sitting on the living room sofa, clutching her cell phone.

"Yeah, I'm fine now."
"I see... If you're fine, then good..."
Mom was oddly restless.

"What's wrong? Did something happen?"
"Huh? No... nothing..."
"............?"
"Never mind that, Natsumi, you're the one who should at least eat some rice gruel. I'll make it."
"Okay, thank you..."
Mom put the phone in her pocket and headed for the kitchen.
This time, I was the one who sat down on the sofa.

It was just right for shaking off the dizziness, but a little cold.
The cooking was done anyway, so I closed the window.
And at once, my heart began to settle.
As if riding along on that, a drowsiness like the one this afternoon tried once again to sit down on my eyelids.
What was it? I was awfully sleepy today.
Even after that long nap earlier.
But I felt like I'd had some scary dream. I didn't really feel like I'd slept.
Maybe after I eat the rice gruel, I'll go to bed early.
No, but I have to take a bath.
"Mom, you haven't cleaned the bath yet, have you? I'll go do it."
"What are you talking about, as wobbly as you are? Sit still and behave."
"But I feel like moving around a little would help. I think April sickness is a matter of mood. I'll go do it."
"Then I'll take you up on it. But just give the bathtub a light wash, okay? Cleaning for more than 3 minutes is forbidden!"
"Okay! I'll go do it!"
Right, this is a matter of mood.
Moping around limp just makes it worse.
I tell myself that every day, but.
Tomorrow, too, I'll probably go through the same thing again.
