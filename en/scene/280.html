Green Eyes ~Deception~

Eden no Sakura Arc [Eden's Cherry] · Shiyo 800-04-06 13:00 火 · 44 boxes

"Sis, I'll leave your food here, okay?"
"Mm... Thanks."
I hadn't eaten anything since dinner the day before, but I didn't feel especially hungry.
Every nerve I had was focused on my brain and my eyes.
At first I'd struggled with the classical Japanese, but as I read, the feel of it from my entrance-exam days came back, and I'd become able to read it without Goto-chan's help.
Goto-chan had gotten photos of several foreign-language documents from me, but all of them seemed to be about 400 years old, and nothing in them looked like a clue.
Most of them were written by our ancestor Ginosuke Niimura, a Westerner naturalized in Japan, and apparently they tell what led him to make Droga, and about Droga experiments using Fujiyoshiro, who would later take the realm.
I'm having her translate those parts right now.
What I'm investigating now is mainly the events that have taken place in the Arata settlement.
In particular, whether anything like the curse-killings ever happened.
History repeats itself; what happens twice will happen a third time. As those sayings go, I can't rule out that the same thing happened before.
My 'learning from the old' takes this angle.
"Sis, aren't you going to eat...?"
Kana called to me again, sounding worried.
"Sis, you look sleepy. Aren't you tired?"
"Mm... I'm fine."
Hearing the word 'tired,' I reflexively grabbed one of the rice balls Kana had brought.
A squishy feeling―
"Sis, that's not a rice ball, it's an ohagi..."
"Huh?"
My hand, so completely convinced it was holding a rice ball, was sticky with bean paste.
"Sis, you really should rest a little..."
"Mm... I'm fine, really. Thanks."
Still holding it in my bare hand, I put the ohagi in my mouth.
A sticky meal, in more ways than one.
"Is there anything I can help with?"
Kana offered, but there was no work I could ask a fifth grader to do.
"Mm... When it's dinnertime, could you bring some up again?"
"Yeah... okay..."
Kana took the empty ohagi plate and went back downstairs.
Was I a little too curt...?
Kana helping out too would be nice if she could, but unfortunately it's far too hard for Kana.
There are only two days left.
I turned the pages with hands that were still just a little sticky.
They say 'history repeats itself,' and sure enough, there seemed to have been more than a few incidents involving Droga.
Especially in Shiyo 340―460 years ago, at the time of the war between the Westerners and the indigenous people―
That time, Droga was apparently used recklessly, and many lives were lost in a terrible battle.
And amid sprays of blood, people turned into the second and third generations, and it grew into instinctive mutual slaughter.
The population of about 100 fell to 50, and it finally ended when the last third-generation person died.
But 12 years later, in Shiyo 352, a girl named Tomi, the only one in the settlement born green-eyed, was apparently seized by that urge to kill when she had her first period.
Resisting it somehow, she took her own life, they say.
I understand how Tomi felt.
I've experienced that urge to kill too.
It's beyond anything like holding back. Your arms and legs just move on their own.
Like an eyelid blinking by reflex when wind hits the eye, I tried to lunge at Mom.
Before she could kill someone by reflex, she thrust her fingers through her eye into her brain and killed herself.
Through this her body was preserved as a mummy, and she came to be worshipped as the goddess Verde.
She became a mummy...?
Was it even possible to make a mummy in Japan back then?
I looked it up on my phone...
I see. Even with the techniques of that time, making a mummy was possible.
Come to think of it, they say there were royals mummified in Africa even back in BC times.
If any of the Westerners back then had that skill, they could have made Tomi into a mummy.
Then is Verde's mummy still preserved somewhere even now?
Auntie might know something. I'll try asking her.
"Mm... Auntie, it's Chigaya."
"Good work, Chigaya-chan. How's it going?"
"Mm... It's going well, if you can call it that. All sorts of facts have come out. And related to that, there's something I want to ask... Auntie, have you ever heard of 'Verde's mummy'?"
"Verde's mummy...? 　Hmm... I wonder. I can't recall it right away..."
"Mm... I see..."
"Have you asked your grandmother or Kotaro-san?"
"Mm... If I can, I'd like to hear it from someone outside the settlement."
"Outside the settlement...? 　Oh, I see what you mean. Well, it never hurts to be careful. I'll think about it a little more on my end too. If I remember anything, I'll let you know. Though I'll be the only one arriving in Arata tonight, ahead of the others."
"Mm... Thanks."
Her reaction was hard to read.
Auntie hiding something from me now, after coming this far, was hard to imagine, but it seemed she couldn't say for sure that Verde's mummy meant nothing to her either.
The phone in my hand vibrated.
Goto-chan had sent a long email.
Inside was a long run of translations of the foreign-language and classical texts.
There was also a note saying she wasn't sure she'd managed an accurate translation of the old foreign language.
Goto-chan said it seemed to be a sort of diary by a man named Ginosuke Niimura.
After moving to the Arata settlement, he had also worked as a researcher of living things, apparently.
In particular, he had been investigating why the creatures of the Arata settlement grew so large.
And the likely cause was...
Many of the mammals living in the settlement had been seen digging up the roots of the Arata cherry and gnawing on them.
Ginosuke suspected there was some secret in the roots of these cherries.
He seemed to have studied the roots by all kinds of methods.
He discovered Droga a few years after moving to the Arata settlement.
He studied every kind of plant and animal, looking for what made this soil so fertile.
Among them were the roots of the Arata cherry.
He burned the Arata cherry roots he no longer needed, it seems―
At that time, several residents working on the burning were apparently seized by a sudden euphoria.
And for twenty or thirty minutes at a stretch, they showed a strength that seemed beyond human.
Ginosuke suspected that the roots had some effect that brought out monstrous strength.
In the end, he succeeded in extracting Droga.
He made it possible to draw human strength out to its limit.
To run that large-scale experiment, Ginosuke handed Droga to a man named Fujiyoshiro.
I see―so that was the trick behind winning out in the Warring States period and ruling the realm.
From here on, it was the translation of the classical text I'd sent yesterday.
Ginosuke never used Droga on himself, but to repay the Arata settlement that had taken him in, he wrote down how to refine it.
So that they would use Droga well and bring eternal peace to this settlement.
Ironically, that was the beginning of the tragedy.
There had been three mutations in all in this settlement, it said.
The first in Shiyo 340, the second in Shiyo 482, the third in Shiyo 768.
From here on, it described the second and later ones.
Shiyo 482: one eye of the young Grand Witch of the time turned green.
And a hideously huge cherry tree grew.
The settlement stood on the brink of destruction, but it was saved at the cost of the young Grand Witch's life.
Shiyo 768: one of the residents found a dried-up corpse in an underground cave.
It was a mummy wearing a Noh mask and red robes, and it was brought up above ground for a time.
From then on, the residents began acting strangely, and a dozen or more people disappeared.
Goto-chan's translation seemed to end there.
Let me sort this out a bit.
The first, the Shiyo 340 affair...
・Killing with Droga breaks out
・Killing by the green-eyed breaks out at the same time
The second, the Shiyo 482 affair...
・One eye of the young Grand Witch of the time turns green
・A giant cherry tree nearly destroys the settlement
・The Grand Witch's life is used to escape the crisis
The third, the Shiyo 768 affair...
・A mummy is found in an underground cave (Verde?)
・After that, the residents start acting strangely
・A year later, a dozen or more people disappear
The more I dig, the stranger the information that comes in.
What bothers me most is the word 'disappearance'.
In the third case, they seem to have disappeared a year after the mummy was found.
And it's not as if everyone disappeared...
To begin with, calling it a disappearance doesn't sit right with me.
People can't physically vanish into nothing, so if they disappeared, they either left the settlement or died.
If they died, it should say they 'died,' not that they 'disappeared'...
So did they leave the settlement as a group after all?
Then where did the group that disappeared go?
And for what purpose did they disappear?
Just when I thought I understood the Green-Eye Curse-Killing and the Arata cherry, a new mystery blocks my way.
And I don't even know whether this has anything to do with the curse-killings.
I'll report this part when the Motoki-cho group arrives and have them think it over with me.
Verde's mummy, and the disappearances.
The only one who knows about these things is me... or maybe not.
If they went to the trouble of fitting such a heavy door on the underground archive room, Grandma and Dad must know what the books say.
............ ............ ............ What?
What does that mean...?
What I just thought... isn't something off about it?
Wait a minute... Something's definitely wrong here, isn't it...!?
Because... at least one of them, Grandma or Dad, knows what's in these books, right?
And I'm the one digging into it now?
Why didn't Grandma or Dad tell me what the books say?
"Damn it...!"
I slam my fist down on the book as hard as I can.
They got me...! 　I've been had...!
So... that's how it is...!
Grandma or Dad, at least one of them, is trying to deceive me.
Here is my guess―
Grandma and Dad both know I'm going through the books in the storehouse right now.
But one of the two must know what books are in the storehouse.
In that case, just telling me even roughly what the books say would get to the truth far faster.
My grounds for saying at least one of the two knows what the books say: that underground door.
I don't know how many hundreds of years ago this storehouse was built, but the door is only about 10 years old.
In other words, Grandma or Dad is guarding this information closely.
And that can only be because they know what the books say.
Then there's only one possibility―
At least one of the two is covering up the truth.
So no matter how much I go through the books here, I'll never reach the truth.
I'd better assume the most important documents have been disposed of or hidden.
It's 3 p.m.
Right now, Grandma and Dad should both be out getting ready for the thirteenth-year memorial service the day after tomorrow.
Here isn't where I really need to be looking.
The Niimura main house...!
