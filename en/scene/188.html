A Fading Star

Saigo no Koe Arc [Last Voice] · Shiyo 800-05-03 21:00 日 · 81 boxes

―The time had gone past 9 p.m.
Dad had gotten drunk and fallen asleep without even getting into the hot spring we'd come all this way for.
Mom and Haruka were having a lively talk about the old days of Haruka's mom.
This was a chance for Haruka and Mom to open up to each other in a new way, so I decided to head to the big bath by myself.
There was no one in the big bath.
It's not often you get the big bath of a luxury inn all to yourself.
For a second I thought about calling Haruka, but right now was an important time for Haruka too.
I went for it and tried swimming in the wide tub.
Swimming as hard as I liked in a space all my own, not worrying about anything, felt wonderful.
"Haah..."
After swimming for a while, I noticed a big sign.
Open-air bath―
I see. An open-air bath.
Coming all the way here and not going would be rude to this inn!
Rude to Haruka's mom!
Rude to the open-air bath!
I eagerly headed for the entrance to the open-air bath.
I could hear the sea, and there were few lights.
A sky full of stars, and a slightly plump half moon.
The location was perfect.
There was no one here either.
It wasn't big enough to swim in, but it was just right for a leisurely soak.
First, without a stitch on, I spread-eagled and caught the wind blowing in off the sea with my whole body.
My flushed body cooled down, nice and refreshing.
A ridiculous pose like this was something I could only pull off now, because no one was watching.
Satisfied with my ridiculous pose, I next jumped, hugged my knees in the same moment, and dove into the tub like an armadillo.
"Haah..."
Once in the tub, I sighed again.
The sea wind on my face was just a little cold, but that was nice too.
I spread-eagled again, floating in the water, and turned my eyes to the sky straight above.
This might be the first time I've seen a sky like this since Arata.
Back then the cherry blossoms were still in bloom, and the moon and stars and a storm of cherry petals colored the black sky.
Are Goto, Erika-chan and Kana-chan, and Shirozaki-san looking at the same starry sky too?
Is everyone doing well?
What became of the incident after that?
Then,
someone seemed to have come in.
It was a portly woman of about 50.
I quickly stopped spread-eagling and sank into the water up to my chin.
"Heave-hooo!"
Shouting as she went, the woman plunged in once with a splash, all the way over her head, then slowly rose back up.
Big waves rose on the surface, and the water surged up to my eyes.
"Ahh, this place is always great, no matter when I come!"
The woman smelled faintly of alcohol.
"Who did you come here with?"
The woman was all smiles as she spoke to me.
"With my family. My dad got drunk and fell asleep... My mom and, um, my big sister are chatting."
"I see, I see. This place is nice no matter when you come."
The woman praised the hot spring once again.
"Who did you come with, ma'am?"
"I'm by myself. Every year when Golden Week comes around, I come here. I'm on my own, so life's easy."
Staying at an inn like this every year... was she a company president or something, like Haruka's mom?
"Hm...?"
The woman's eyes went to my shoulder.
A scar still ran from my shoulder down to my chest.
Supposedly it'll fade over time, though.
"Let's see, how about I do a reading for you?"
"A reading...?"
The woman turned to face me squarely.
"I may not look it, but I make my living telling fortunes. People say I'm right a lot."
A fortune-teller...
To make a living from that alone, no, to come here every year, she must be pretty popular.
The woman studied my face intently.
Not asking me anything in particular, not reading my palm. Just studying my face, intently.
"Miss, you've got quite a lot of good luck. You've been blessed with the people around you, and you've lived a happy life up to now, wanting for nothing. Your family, your friends, they all just love you."
"Oh... yes. Ehe, I guess so."
Being told that made me plainly happy.
"But that's a happiness you got by a miracle, from out of life's many branch points. Supported by all kinds of people, and supporting all kinds of people yourself. Rather than a miracle, I suppose you'd call it a happiness you won for yourselves."
Is she talking about 10 years ago, and Arata?
If so, she's pretty much right.
I am who I am today thanks to everyone.
If even one thing had gone wrong...
"Oh, sorry, sorry, no use talking about the past. I ought to tell you about the future. Let's see... your future is... Oh, do you want to hear it? Some people don't like hearing this kind of thing."
"Please. I'd like to hear it."
"Hmm... Before that, let me tell you a little about my work."
The woman, who had been staring at my face, slowly turned her gaze to the sky.
"People... all die sooner or later. Sometimes people facing death come to me for advice. They've gotten sick, so how can they be saved? They're about to go bankrupt and suicide's the only way out, so what should they do? To put it plainly, fortune-telling can't save anyone."
"If they'd come to me sooner, maybe there would've been a chance of saving them, but... People like that all come once it's too late."
It isn't only curious people who visit a fortune-teller. People who truly want to be saved come too.
So that meant this woman had seen plenty of people on the brink of crises in their lives like that.
"So here's what I tell people like that. People die sooner or later. Human beings have all been sentenced to death by God. But God does let us choose one thing: what kind of end we meet. How do you face up to that? By this point, it's less fortune-telling than life counseling."
"'It wasn't a bad life. So that you'll be able to think that, please do the best you can with what you can do now.' That's really all I can tell them. Not that I think many people are saved by it. I can give advice with fortune-telling, but whether life itself changes is up to the person."
The woman, who had been gazing up at the heavens, looked into my eyes with a serious face.
"Miss, listen carefully. In the near future, you'll lose one of the people important to you. Maybe even today or tomorrow."
My heart skipped a beat.
With what had just happened in Arata, my anxiety swelled more than usual.
"But it's not all that unhappy a story. It's what's bound to happen. People all die sooner or later. That's all it is. And it's something that could happen to anyone, at any time."
"Is there... no way to prevent it?"
"Should you prevent it?"
"Huh...?"
"Say it's the best end that person could wish for. Would you still prevent it?"
This was getting too philosophical for me to really follow.
It was the first time I'd heard a way of thinking that affirmed death.
"I don't know... But I don't want to lose people who are important to me."
I know people die someday.
But...
"At your age, it'd be hard. No, it's hard for anyone, even me. But there are things you only understand once you're facing death. My parents are gone now, and my husband passed on when he was young too. But what kind of end you get is different for everyone. Some people say they lived without regrets, and some people can't even tell that they've died."
I remembered Haruka's dad, who died protecting me.
I can't even imagine what he felt at the very end.
Haruka's dad...?
I feel like I met him somewhere recently.
He died more than 12 years ago, and yet just recently, somewhere...
"Um, excuse me... I feel like I'm forgetting something really important. Can you tell anything...?"
"Now that's a vague question. Well, all right, since I'm at it, let me do a little reading."
The woman stared into my eyes again.
I stared into the woman's eyes too.
"Huh? Miss, have we met somewhere...? 　Ever been to Tokyo?"
"Tokyo...? 　No, not even once..."
"Then I've got the wrong person. Well, sure, the face is completely different... It's just, I once saw someone who had an aura like yours, or something along those lines. A young person, Japanese but somehow not very Japanese-looking."
Someone in Tokyo who didn't look Japanese...
Could it be...
Why was that?
The sound of the waves, which had been filling my ears until then, grew farther and farther away.
"See, I gave that person some advice once. 'There's someone close to you who'll deceive you, so be careful.' That's what I told them."
"By any chance, did that person...
have odd eyes, one blue and one green?"
"Hmm, now that you mention it, I think so. Huh? So you know that person?"
"Yes, there's someone I have in mind. But that person's face is nothing like mine, and I don't think we have a similar aura either..."
When I'd said that much, the woman looked into my eyes even harder.
"Hmmm... Miss, it may be a good thing you met me. What you've forgotten is deeply tied to that odd-eyed woman. That person and you are very much alike. How should I put it... Your constitution, I guess you'd call it. Like 'constitutions that cannot coexist.'"
She said we were very much alike, and then 'constitutions that cannot coexist'? That was clearly saying the opposite.
What she was saying just didn't sit right with me.
"You have two choices. Learn the truth and be unhappy, or turn your eyes away from the truth and be happy. That odd-eyed woman is deeply involved in it too. What you've forgotten is a defensive instinct that worked without you knowing― it's not something you're meant to remember. No, to be precise, it's all right to remember what you've forgotten. The question is what you decide once you have. Depending on that, the fates of you and those around you will change a great deal."
"So remembering itself is okay, then?"
"That's right. What you've forgotten is this: 'You must not learn the truth about a certain thing.' So if you want happiness, then even if a great mystery stands in your way, you mustn't pursue it any further. But whether you do is up to you."
The first thing that came to mind was the truth of the Arata curse-killings.
In the end, that incident had come to its close with many mysteries left unsolved.
It's already in the past, and yet knowing the truth of it means unhappiness...?
By now, I could clearly remember meeting Ojisan in a dream.
The promise I made with Ojisan. That I must not uncover the truth of the curse-killings―
And the reason: knowing the truth means unhappiness.
"I... No, I understand. It's better not to know the truth, right?"
"Yes, that's best. You're a good, honest girl."
The woman gave a gentle smile.
I made a promise to Ojisan.
I have no intention of disregarding the promise Ojisan entrusted to me.
All the more now that this woman has told me so too.
"And another thing... This isn't a very nice story either, but... Mind if I tell you?"
"Yes..."
"There are a few people important to you, I think. One of them will lose something important."
"Someone important to me will lose something important...? 　When someone might die as soon as today or tomorrow, there's even more...?"
"For example, you said you had an older sister, didn't you?"
"Yes."
"I mean that older sister might lose something important. Oh, the older sister is just an example. I mean someone that close and important to you."
"Something important... What could it be...?"
"What that person will lose is far too big. So big that they may lose themselves and never be able to come back."
"That much...!?"
"What they'll lose, and why, that much I can't tell."
"Can't I be a support for them after they lose it...?"
"That's the thing... You can't help that person."
"Why not? I want to help in any way I can..."
"............ Miss. Look up at the sky."
"The sky...?"
In the sky, the same countless stars as before.
They shone so brightly that no word but 'mysterious' could be found for them, showing themselves off against the pitch-black darkness to us in the world below.
"Beyond that sky are the people who have died until now. They're watching us from the other world. Do you know how many human beings have died up to now?"
"How many people have died up to now...?"
"106 billion, they say."
"That many...!?"
"Oh, you think that's a lot? 　People split two ways on this, the ones who think it's a lot and the ones who think it's few. That many people are watching us from beyond the sky. Among them are plenty of your ancestors too. But not a single one of them will help you. There are things that feelings alone can't help."
I didn't really understand what she meant.
Could helping someone important to me really be blocked by an idea that grand?
"Someone important to you loses something important, loses their reason to live, loses themselves. Then they shut themselves away in a world of their own and can never come out again. Fates like that exist too."
In other words, someone who's shut themselves away in their own world might as well be living in a different world.
So was she trying to say I couldn't possibly help them?
"If that person had even a little courage... then they might be able to come out, but for that, they need someone who can give them courage. That might be someone who lives in a world different from ours. Someone that far away."
The talk had gotten so big that I was starting to lose track.
"Sorry, miss. Beyond this, even I can't tell. But you might want to at least brace yourself."
"That fortune... will it be right?"
"Some fortunes are right, some fortunes are wrong. If nothing happens, there's no need to worry about it. If something does happen, my words might be of some use. I'd say it's best to keep them in mind just about that much."
"Under... stood..."
Then my head started to go fuzzy.
I'd gotten so caught up talking that I'd stayed in the bath too long.
"I'm going to get out now. Thank you for everything."
"Mm-hm. I hope it turns out to be a good life."
"Yes."
"Miss... Have a good life."
―When I got back to the room, it was already 10:30 p.m.
Maybe I stayed in the bath a little too long...
"Welcome back. You sure took a while."
Haruka was lying down, watching TV.
"Yeah, I got to talking with someone who was in there with me."
"Huh. Staying in that long, you must've had a really interesting talk."
"Sort of..."
Interesting? I wasn't so sure. It was so difficult I didn't understand even half of it.
Dad was fast asleep.
Mom seemed to have fallen asleep watching TV too.
"I talked with Obasan, and we decided we'd stay the way we've always been."
Saying that, Haruka turned her eyes to Mom's face.
"This person is the one who gave birth to me, huh... It still doesn't really feel real, though. But having two moms... I don't really get it, but it's kinda nice."
Then Haruka's phone rang.
"Who's that? At this hour..."
Haruka picked up her phone.
"It's my mom. Hello? What's up? 　Uh-huh... Uh-huh... What!?"
