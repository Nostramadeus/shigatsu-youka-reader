Fifteen minutes into the search.
Plenty of time for the two of us to go through everything.
I went through Kana's desk, and Kana through the closet, but...
"Hmm, not even Daddy's dirty magazines or dirty DVDs are turning up... I guess it's all online these days."
Are Dad and Grandma innocent after all?
That would be good news, but it doesn't mean they're cleared of suspicion yet.
All we know is it isn't in this room, and there are plenty of other places to search.
"Sis, why don't you take a bath? 　Your clothes really stink of the storehouse."
"Mm... Do I smell?"
I held my sleeve to my nose.
I caught a faint smell, something like mold, something like dust.
Kana, with her keen eyes, nose, and ears, can probably pick up the smell even from a little way off.
"Mm... Maybe I'll change. Sorry, but Kana, could you tidy up the closet?"
"Aww? That's a pain..."
"Mm... The one who searched the closet was you, right? 　Everything has to go back where it was. I'm counting on you."
"Okaaay..."
This was the room I used when I lived in Arata.
It's still kept as my room, but Kana will end up using it before long.
I pulled some clothes, whatever came to hand, out of a cardboard box.
I'd like a bath, but I might get dirty again, so that can wait.
Huh? In that case... is there any point in changing?
Once I'm back in the storehouse, the fresh clothes will pick up the smell too.
I put the clothes I'd pulled out back in the box.
I don't like being dirty, but given what I'm doing right now, I'll have to put up with a little.
Tonight I'll surely be told to eat dinner at home, so I'll take care of the bath and changing then.
Besides, I don't want to sit down to dinner in front of everyone looking grubby.
When I got back to the room, Kana was glancing restlessly around.
The things she'd taken out of the closet still hadn't been put away.
"Mm... Kana, all this stuff―"
"Shh! Quiet!"
A curt reply that cut me off, then a fixed glare at the ceiling from Kana.
As if chasing a mosquito in midair, her eyes even seemed to be following something I couldn't see.
"Sis... it still smells..."
"Mm... I haven't changed yet. My clothes might just get dirty again..."
"No! Even after you left the room, Sis, it kept smelling of the storehouse!"
"Huh?"
"It's in this room―the thing you're looking for, Sis."
Don't tell me Kana sniffed out the smell of the books?
"It's been weird since yesterday. This room smelled a little moldy. I figured it was because Daddy went all over the place getting ready for the thirteenth-year memorial service, but this smell is the same as that storehouse."
Kana kept staring at the ceiling.
"Mm... The space above the ceiling...?"
"Probably..."
"Mm... Kana, get on my shoulders."
I put Kana on my shoulders and had her feel around the ceiling.
Kana sniffed and sniffed, and knocked on the ceiling, tap, tap.
To look at, they were perfectly ordinary ceiling boards, but...
"Mm! 　The sound's different only here. See, there's something up there."
Only where Kana knocked was the sound a little heavier.
"Mm... Is there a seam in the board? Can it be opened?"
"Hmm... No seam at all. I don't think it opens."
"Mm... Then maybe you can get above the ceiling from somewhere else?"
"Maybe there's a secret window in the roof?"
"Mm... With Dad's build, climbing onto the second-floor roof is out of the question. Somewhere in the second-floor rooms, there must be a space that leads above this ceiling."
"Okay! Now this is turning into a real investigation!"
Kana was delighted, but has she forgotten that the one being investigated is her own father...?
"Mm... Kana, you check here and the guest room. I'll check the hallway and the other rooms."
"Okay, okaaay!"
"Before that, though, put the closet back in order."
"Tch..."
First, the second-floor hallway.
Everyone passes through here all the time, so if anything big were off, someone would notice.
And yet...
The ceiling was old and darkened, so if anyone had cut into it, the marks would stand out.
I poked at it with a broom, but these weren't boards that would come loose easily.
Let me try the next room.
This room usually sits empty.
It's a cramped little three-mat room, where Mom used to do her foreseeing long ago.
How many years has it been since I came in here?
A closet built into the cramped room―
I opened it, but there was nothing inside.
Above it was the top cupboard.
That should be empty too, but...
The moment I opened it, I felt the faintest breath of air.
In this sealed room, a draft from a top cupboard that should have been just as sealed...
Don't tell me... back in there...!?
At my height, I couldn't see all the way to the back of the top cupboard.
"Mm... Kana, come here a sec!"
"I'm not done tidying yet!"
"Mm... That can wait!"
"Jeez, you're such a slave driver..."
"Yes, yes, what can I do for you?"
"Mm... Shine this phone into the back of the top cupboard for me. There was a draft coming from in here."
"............ Yeah... it smells..."
Kana took my phone and climbed into the top cupboard without even needing my shoulders.
"Mm... Well? Can you get above the ceiling?"
"No way, no way! This is all one solid board too! 　But this board's really new!? Did someone replace it?"
"Mm... Can you break it?"
"I'm allowed to break it?"
"Mm... Go ahead. I'll take responsibility."
"Got it!"
From the top cupboard came the echo of the ceiling being pounded hard.
With Kana's fists, a few blows should be enough.
Then, once again, air blew into the room.
Stronger than before.
"Whoa! It stinks! 　I'm going in for a bit!"
It seemed she'd made a hole.
It was a real help that Kana was small and athletic.
If I went up above the ceiling with my weight, the ceiling boards would surely give way.
While she's at it, let me tidy up Dad's room.
I tried to put everything back where it had been, but since Kana was the one who took it all out, I couldn't help guessing.
But come to think of it, maybe there had been no need to sneak around at all.
If Dad is innocent, an explanation will settle it, and if he's guilty, I just have to confront him right away.
"Sis, you there?"
It was Kana's voice, from above the ceiling.
I knocked on the spot Kana had said was suspicious earlier.
"Mm... Kana, this is the spot you said sounded heavy."
"Hang on a sec... Let's see..."
Now... Is Dad guilty... or is he innocent...
"Sis... they're here. Books that smell like the storehouse―several of them."
"Mm... Okay, bring them back down."
Dad, or else Grandma, is guilty after all, it seems.
"So you're reading them out here after all."
"Mm... At home, it'd be a pain to hide them when Dad and Grandma get back. Kana, are you going to read them too?"
"Well, of course I'm curious! You'll tell me, right!?"
"Mm... I don't mind, but it's bound to be difficult. 　I'm not sure even I'll understand it..."
"Hnng... if it's hard even for you, Sis, it'd be hard for me too..."
"Mm... Then, Kana, you go back to the house. If we're both shut up in the storehouse, people will think it's strange."
"I'll do that... But tell me all about it later, okay?"
She seemed to accept it, if reluctantly.
I don't mind Kana knowing what's in them, but if I explain every little thing, I'll run out of time.
Now, there are three books here.
The books hidden in the attic―in look and in smell, they certainly seem the same as the ones in the storehouse basement.
But only these were deliberately hidden in the attic.
Whether Dad hid them, or Grandma hid them, or the two of them hid them together, I can't say for sure.
The first― The title: 'The Ecology of Shigatsu Youka.'
What on earth is this... Is it read 'Shigatsu Youka,' I wonder?
The second― 'The Fierce Sound and the Green-Eye Curse-Killing'―this one looks like an old text.
I understand 'Green-Eye Curse-Killing,' but what is 'the Fierce Sound'...
The third― 'What Became of the Vanished'―this looks like classical Japanese, but close to modern too.
Right, the second one, 'The Fierce Sound and the Green-Eye Curse-Killing,' I'll send to Goto-chan to translate.
It doesn't have many pages, so photographing and sending it is no great trouble.
Taking the photos, I decided which book I should read.
'The Ecology of Shigatsu Youka,' or 'What Became of the Vanished'?
The former about 70 years ago, the latter about 130.
The latter seems to have been written first, so maybe I'll start with that one.
The classical Japanese of this era isn't all that hard.
What Became of the Vanished― In Shiyo 668, 132 years ago, there was a mass disappearance in the Arata settlement, and the residents who remained seem to have looked into it.
According to them...
'In Shiyo 669, the spring after the mass disappearance, we noticed that several dozen unfamiliar cherry trees had appeared around the Arata settlement. They had suddenly appeared in places where, until the year before, there had been no sign of any tree at all. As if they had bloomed in full every year, the great cherry trees proudly flaunted their blossoms.'
'The number of cherry trees that suddenly appeared was about 30. Strangely, it is almost the same as the number of residents who vanished last year.'
'For our inquiry, we dug up one of the newly grown trees by the roots. As we did so, clothing and a skeleton came up, tangled in the roots. So it is true. These cherry trees grew suddenly, using the residents as a seedbed. These trees are no cherry trees. They are something else, something that looks exactly like a cherry.'
'Since ancient times, it is said, these cherry-tree-like things that suddenly appear have been called 'Shigatsu Youka.' An eerie flower blooms in the month of death. Those of the Arata settlement, barring accident or illness, die almost without fail in April―shigatsu. That is why it is the month of death.'
So what this means is, when residents of the Arata settlement vanish, they turn into cherry trees?
And not only that―the trees spring up all at once and grow big enough to bloom in full.
Put this together with the experiment report I read earlier...
The cherry trees that suddenly appear are not trees but mushrooms.
And the spores get inside the human body, spreading the infection while changing their nature.
Spores are something like seeds, so once a certain generation comes, the cherry blooms using humans as its seedbed―is that it?
I see. It adds up.
Besides, as the residents' register showed, the people of the Arata settlement really do all die in April.
Worse, before the curse-killings of these last 10 years, the old people used to kill themselves in April, as if to keep up the record of April deaths.
So from that era on, there was this bizarre endemic disease of dying in April.
There's more to 'What Became of the Vanished.'
'Indeed, if we think of it this way, can we not solve nearly all the mysterious phenomena surrounding this Arata settlement? Until now this inexplicable phenomenon has been dismissed as curses and yokai, but we live in an age when all Japan is expected to Westernize. It is time we broke free of this myth-mindedness and unraveled the mysteries of the Arata settlement realistically.'
'As one part of this, we will send a representative from the Arata settlement to Tokyo City. We will have him carry Droga and present it to those who hold power. If the right person can be found, research with ample funds and facilities should be possible, but there is no knowing when the mystery of Droga will be solved― Even if it takes 100 years, it ought to be unraveled across the generations.'
What can be inferred from this is that the residents of Arata somehow got close to the powerful of the day.
About 60 years after this era, Arata's cherry trees were taken away by some research group or other.
So it took a full 60 years for serious research to finally begin.
Thus a scientific analysis of Droga was carried out in an age when science had advanced to some degree.
It's guesswork, but the story does connect, more or less.
―Mail from Goto-chan―the translation of 'The Fierce Sound and the Green-Eye Curse-Killing' I sent her just now.
That's Goto-chan for you―fast work.
"Mm...?"
Before the translation, there was a comment from Goto-chan.
Goto-chan's translation mails usually came with comments that had almost nothing subjective in them, but this time Goto-chan's own opinion seemed to be mixed in.
'Chiga-nee, do you know about the apocalyptic sound? 　What this book describes is most likely that. I'm sending a link to a video, so please watch it.'
Pasted in were these words from Goto-chan and the URL of a video site.
First, let me watch the video.
Just a video of an ordinary night scene, with some great sound ringing out over it.
Like a big machine running inside a factory, like a giant old iron door moving.
A sound hard to describe, but at the very least not a pleasant one.
If this is unedited footage, you could call it a very strange sound indeed.
If Goto-chan went to the trouble of sending this, she must mean that, in 'The Fierce Sound and the Green-Eye Curse-Killing,' 'the Fierce Sound' refers to this.
Apocalyptic sound―let me try a quick search on the net with that.
―About 100,000 hits.
Known to those who know it―about that level of fame, I suppose.
Still, if there are even aggregator sites and analysis sites, I should be able to get fairly objective information.
video: 悪天候.wmv
A phenomenon of strange sounds, like sirens or scraping metal, observed around the world in recent years.
The cause is unknown, but the deeply unpleasant sound, the kind that stirs people's anxiety, became a topic.
That sound is called the apocalyptic sound.
Supposedly, it is a sound that announces the ruin of the world.
video: 悪天候.wmv
Ridiculous... that's the kind of interpretation you get in an occult gossip piece.
No... Goto-chan warned me that kind of thinking is dangerous.
Even if it seems ridiculous, it's not information I can ignore.
This is really starting to sound like religion.
Speaking of religion...
video: 悪天候.wmv
The ancestors of this Arata settlement included Westerners, who had a faith of their own, they say.
Traces of it remain even now, and every house has at least one copy of its scripture.
Come to think of it, there was one down in the basement here too.
There might be a hint in it―let me go get it.
