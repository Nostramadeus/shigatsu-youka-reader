The Gateway to Paradise

Eden no Sakura Arc [Eden's Cherry] · Shiyo 309-12-24 15:30 #VALUE! · 49 boxes

A letter sent to his lord by someone who strayed into a settlement deep in the mountains

As kitchen steward, I'd managed to show even my lord what I could do.
With this, my rise in the world was sure to pick up more and more speed.
A farmer's son, and my old man a foot soldier.
For a guy like me to rise, I needed a master who looked at ability, not pedigree.
Before I entered the service of the Ouda house, things were truly awful.
I used to serve Motoyoshi Magawa-dono.
Called the finest warlord in the land, Motoyoshi Magawa-dono― the Magawa house was an old-line noble family descended from the Minato clan.
With a pedigree like that, the retainers and everyone else were all hung up on pedigree.
Not ability but which house you were born into decided your fate from then on.
Even so, I didn't let it get me down, and I worked like mad to be useful to the Magawa house.
Well, to be exact, I wasn't serving the Magawa house itself, but as a retainer of a retainer of a retainer of Motoyoshi-dono.
Still, no matter how hard I fought on my own, there was no way they'd recognize it― and I quietly left the Magawa house.
I think I was around 18 then.
My lord was good enough to take me on as a servant, and I worked harder than ever, set on proving useful here if nowhere else.
My lord was just the man I'd thought he was.
He didn't care about rank. He judged purely on ability, and was good enough to give each man a fitting post.
Kitchen steward or whatever else, I pushed through and did everything I could.
Having saved on firewood, water, everything, and succeeded in slashing the kitchen's expenses, I was honored with words of praise from my lord.
But this wasn't all I had, not by a long shot.
More, much more, I was going to show them what I could do.
Then one day, in December of Shiyo 309.
I had a chance to meet up with a friend from my days serving Magawa-dono.
Of course, I couldn't tell my lord anything like that.
The Magawa house was growing powerful enough to swallow the Ouda house, and there was no telling what next year might bring.
Being taken for a Magawa spy was the last thing I needed.
"Hey, Yoshiro! Long time no see!"
"Hey, Hokichi! Long time no see!"
We traded the same greeting back, parrot-fashion, and laughed together, gahaha.
"What's going on? A retainer of Magawa-dono going out of his way to come into the Ouda domain, of all things. What do you think'll happen if you're found?"
"Haha! Say that, and you won't get off easy either, letting me into the Ouda domain!?"
Hokichi laughed cheerfully, but he'd come to see me knowing the danger.
I knew he hadn't just come by for fun.
"Yoshiro... how's the Ouda house?"
"How is it, huh... My lord is a strict man. But for that very reason, he keeps a sharp eye on how you work. He looks at your work, not your rank. It's the perfect place for me to rise."
"I see..."
Hokichi's cheerful face began to cloud over, and his words came slower.
Just as I thought, this was no small matter.
"Say, Yoshiro... would you think about coming back to the Magawa house?"
"Huh?"
"Quit the Ouda house and come back to the Magawa house."
"So that's it... The Ouda house is going to fall soon, isn't it?"
"Yeah..."
"When will that be?"
"Probably around early summer next year. Lord Motoyoshi means to go to the Western Capital, where the Emperor's castle stands. On the way, he's bound to pass through this Ouda domain."
"I see... Ouda's domain―enter it without leave, and Magawa has invaded Ouda. And if Ouda lets that go by without lifting a hand, it's surrendered to Magawa in all but name. If Ouda is to go on being Ouda, it has to attack Magawa when that happens."
"Exactly. So... come back. There's no way Ouda can beat Magawa."
"I...! I'm a farmer's son, and my old man's a foot soldier...! 　You know a guy like me can't make a name for himself at Magawa! 　The Ouda house is the only place I can make my mark!"
"Yoshiro... I know how you feel. But... so what if you never rise? With the Magawa house you'd be secure for good―"
"Don't talk nonsense! I entered the service of the Ouda house to become someone big in this country! 　Magawa―before I'd go back there, I'd sooner fall along with the Ouda house!"
"Yoshiro..."
"Sorry, Hokichi... I know you came all this way because you're worried about me. But..."
"............"
Our conversation went no further than that.
The Ouda house was fated to fall in half a year or so.
I'd finally found my place to belong. Like I was going to just sit there and keep quiet...!
What could I... what could I do!? What was it!?
I'm the kitchen steward!
What I can do, as kitchen steward, is― what, exactly...?
―Shiyo 310, early spring.
The cold had only just begun to ease, ever so slightly, and the buds on the trees were swelling.
I went straight to my lord with a plea.
I wanted to find a food that would make the soldiers strong, and help the Ouda house expand its power.
Food is the very source of human strength, something no one can do without.
Don't get me wrong, though: I'm the kitchen steward, not a cook.
Managing the kitchen is my job, and making meals and getting in ingredients is the cooks' job.
So searching for a food that makes men strong is a little outside my duties.
But if things stay like this―
My lord recognized what I'd achieved so far and was good enough to grant me leave to travel the provinces for a month, and no more.
I could hardly tell him that Magawa was going to attack, so I stuck to the pretext that I wanted to find something to give the soldiers vigor.
I had no attendants. I'd be doing it all on my own.
I choose my road by gut feeling.
If I'm going anywhere, it's uncharted land where hardly anyone has ever set foot.
Otherwise I'll never find anything that could overturn the Magawa house.
Whatever you can find where people live won't amount to much.
A food that builds strength the instant you eat it, and turns men into soldiers like demon warriors.
Something that convenient can't possibly exist. I know that much.
It's like trying to grab a cloud, but if I don't grab that cloud, it's all over.
I won't say I had no doubts as I took the first step.
The me that said this was all pointless.
The me that said I'd never survive unless I did it.
Those two opposing thoughts had slowed my feet, just a little.
But turning back was out of the question.
Coming back with nothing at all would drag the name of my lord, who'd allowed my selfish request, through the mud.
............ ............ This is...
A little way out from the castle, I come to a big hollow.
Ringed by high ground, and then an inlet―
Magawa is sure to pass through here on the way to the Western Capital.
If only we could launch a surprise attack here...
Just then, a clever idea comes to me.
What if I hired merchants here and had them serve up good food?
Magawa would surely stop to rest, too.
All right, I'll remember this place.
If I remember right, this place is―
Taruhazama.
―All I have with me is a bare minimum of money and a short sword.
I'll wash my clothes in whatever river I come across, and if the cords of my straw sandals snap, I'll fix them myself.
If bandits find me, I run for all I'm worth.
At a glance it's a reckless journey, but doing nothing at all would be far more reckless, no doubt about it.
My lord must have sensed that somehow, too.
But if he let me prepare no more than this, it's to see whether I truly have the will to die as a servant of the Ouda house.
And whether I can pull off a big job with preparations short of even the bare minimum.
My lord means to test me, too.
This is a rare chance to rise.
My first night finds me in the mountains past Taruhazama.
I'm a bit tired, as you'd expect, so I'll spend the night here.
Still, I figured bandits would jump me, but these mountains are awfully peaceful.
They were supposed to be a lot more lawless before, though.
Of course. My lord is seeing to the upkeep of this land too, without missing a thing, and his idea is to make it a lively land where merchants come and go freely.
For that, having bandits and their like around would defeat the whole purpose.
Starting with work like this on the roads, my lord seems to be pushing steadily toward his dream.
My dreams are big too! When it comes to the size of my dreams, I won't lose even to my lord!
When I passed through Magawa lands, I deliberately took the long way round and visited poor fishing villages and mountain villages.
It was to find some food the world didn't know about, food brimming with power.
But all the villagers served me was humble stuff, small fry and wild greens and the like.
No, to them it was a feast, and I was grateful for the food too, but it wasn't what I was looking for.
Just tasting good wasn't enough.
The food has to fill the soldiers with power.
Pit vipers or bear paws would do, but there aren't nearly enough to supply the Ouda house's 2,000 soldiers.
It's a lot to ask, but something that fills you with power, and that I can get enough of for 2,000 men―
I wander around in search of something that dreamlike.
All of a sudden, I look up at a cherry tree.
Soon it'll be the season when the cherries bloom.
The buds are swelling, and the sun feels just a little hot.
Motoyoshi Magawa's attack is about three months away, I'd say.
After going around a number of fishing and mountain villages in the Magawa domain, I notice something.
Within the Magawa house's lands, a domain law has been laid down.
In other words, a law issued for inside the Magawa domain.
A house as powerful as Magawa would have full control of the lands well in place, and the rice tax and other dues would be getting paid too.
So everything you can get around here is also delivered to the Magawa house as tax.
And it isn't just Magawa.
The provinces I'm about to head into are ruled by warlords like Takeda and Nanjo.
There's no point in bringing back something another daimyo owns.
Should I search around the borders between domains, where their eyes reach least?
The road will literally be a steep one, though.
I changed course to the north side of the range and trekked through the steep mountains.
Crossing a range that had to be a ri high was no ordinary feat.
In early spring there were still few wild greens, and bears that had woken from hibernation hungry were roaming around.
Frogs and snakes gone thin from hibernation filled my belly, but I couldn't keep living like this for long.
I had to find what I was after, and get back soon.
"Hm?"
From under the melting snow, a tree branch, white as the snow itself, is peeking out.
White branches are rare.
I've hardly seen any around here, but could it be a white birch?
I grab the branch, stuck fast in the snow and dirt, and give it a hard yank.
"Wh-what on earth is this...!"
It took me a good long while to realize it was an animal's bone.
After all, it was a bone as huge as a monster's.
Was this from around a deer's ribs?
It was a lot sturdier than my leg.
If these mountains are crawling with monsters like this, there might be some incredible food here.
All right, I'll search this area a little more carefully.
This deep in the mountains, not even bandits will live here, and I can hope to find things nobody knows about yet.
Then, a rustling of bamboo grass leaves being pushed aside.
Could it be prey already!?
What burst out was a huge black shape, so big I took it for a house.
But I couldn't make sense of it as a boar.
Next to the boars I knew, it was the difference between a cat and a tiger.
And it robbed me, a man out looking for prey, of even the thought of hunting this prey.
Ah, there had to be some wonderful, strength-giving food here after all.
Seeing this boar, which you could only call monstrous, made me sure of it.
My memory ends here.
