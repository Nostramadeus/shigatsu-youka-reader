The Day the Witch Was Born

Meicho Arc [Clear Sign] · Shiyo 800-04-07 17:10 火 · 72 boxes

"Still, I can't say it..."
When I was in kindergarten, my family and Natsumi's went to Central Park to view the cherry blossoms.
I ran around with Natsumi, both of us holding balloons, while our dads, drinks in hand, enjoyed their day off.
And then that strong wind blew―
Central Park had been calm and sunny with spring, when all of a sudden a gale tore through it.
Natsumi's balloon was carried off by that wind and got caught in Central Park's goshinboku, and―
Natsumi burst into tears.
"Natsumi, don't cry. You can have my balloon."
Even so, Natsumi didn't stop crying.
Seeing all that, my dad climbed the goshinboku, and―
My dad fell from the tree trying to get the balloon caught in the goshinboku, and died.
The scene was gruesome. As he fell, Dad had his neck run through by the park fence.
Dad's spraying blood drenched Natsumi, who was below, from head to toe, and from that time on, Natsumi was afraid of cherry blossoms.
She was 4 at the time, and Natsumi doesn't seem to remember it.
But I remember it, faintly.
The wound in his neck couldn't hold his weight and kept tearing wider, until at last the blood sprayed out.
My dad being dead is something Natsumi still doesn't know, either.
The story is that he was transferred overseas and went alone.
My mom and Natsumi's parents apparently decided to hide it that way, but...
Just how long do they plan to keep it hidden?
Dad had only just died when Mom and I left Motoki-cho and moved to Megasawa City in Iizawa Prefecture.
Losing Dad had made Mom ill, and it was so she could recuperate.
There's a hospital there that specializes in mental illness, and Dad's hometown, the Arata settlement, is nearer from there than from Motoki-cho.
Mom was hospitalized, and I went into a children's home near the hospital.
A lot of the kids at the home were in situations like mine, and I fit in right away.
But sleeping away from Mom was hard on me, a child not yet in school.
Mom spent all her time gazing out of her hospital room at the cherry trees in leaf.
Dad's death seemed to have been a terrible shock to her.
I wanted to be some help to Mom somehow.
I wanted her to get well soon, so we could go back to Motoki-cho―
To cheer Mom up, I cast charms on her that I'd read about in picture books.
Only at those times would Mom turn to me with a smile.
That alone made me happy.
I looked through all kinds of picture books at the home, searching for charms.
It was around then that I came to know.
That among charms, there are "curses" that make trouble for people.
By the time I was starting elementary school, Mom's illness had gotten a lot better.
Mom was discharged, and we moved into a run-down apartment building called Nanami-so.
Old apartment or not, this would bring our old life back.
Once Mom was properly well, we'd go back to Motoki-cho, and I'd be able to play with Natsumi.
That was the hope that filled my heart, but...
"Hey, Mikogami! You pray to some weird god, don't you!?"
"What! This one's family is in that evil cult!?"
"Ugh... we'd better not get involved..."
"H-hey. You shouldn't bully Mikogami-san..."
I stood up for a kid in my new class who was being bullied.
Because of that, before long I became the target of the bullying.
Kids bully where adults can't see.
On top of that, even if I alone went to the teacher, if the great majority of the others said "That's not true," the teacher would also decide I was imagining things.
To make matters worse― this I only found out later― the parent of a kid who was bullying me was apparently a PTA officer.
The kind of parent people call a monster parent.
Things never took a turn for the better.
In front of Mom, I tried to act cheerful.
Mom had to get well soon, that was why.
One day, just before I started second grade.
"Hey, hey, Mom! Natsumi's gonna be in elementary school soon too, right!?"
Even after we parted in Motoki-cho, my best friend was still Natsumi.
But whenever I talked about Natsumi, Mom's face would always go blank.
"I see... Natsumi-chan's starting elementary school already. Haruka, is school fun?"
"............ Yeah, it's fun!"
"Really... is it really fun?"
"............ Yeah... it's fun..."
"............"
Mom was looking at me as if she could see right through me.
Those eyes of Mom's, always so gentle, scared me a little.
"Haruka, if you're having fun, then that's fine. But if anything hurts, make sure you tell me."
"............ Yeah..."
With that one word, my smile turned into tears.
Right now, the only one I could really rely on was Mom.
I told Mom about the bullying.
"I see. Haruka, you're a kind girl. Helping a child who's being bullied is a very good thing to do. If that kind of thing is happening, cast a charm. Haruka, you're good at charms, aren't you?"
Mom gave me a book a few days later.
It was a children's book called 'The World of Curses,' yet it was a strangely stark, rugged book.
When I flipped through it, I found ways of summoning demons and cursing people to death written in it, at a children's-book level, but there.
"Thank you, Mom. But charms are for helping you, Mom."
"I see. What a good girl you are, Haruka."
That's what I said, but inside I was very interested.
If curses could make the bullying go away and help Mom get better―
―A few days later, my patience reached its limit.
No, for me, with the weapon of curses in my hands, maybe it was just the natural course of things.
The method was simple. Write the person's name on a paper doll, and spit on it.
Then offer a prayer to your departed ancestors.
With that, the two who bullied me worst would disappear from in front of me.
"Mom, where's some plain white paper and scissors?"
"Plain white paper and scissors? Are you doing a charm?"
"Uh-uh, no. My charms are for making you better, Mom."
"I see..."
That time too, Mom looked at me as if she could see right through me.
But I played dumb about the paper dolls to the very end.
It was true that I used charms to help Mom.
Only, that was the goal, and as for what the means were―there was no way I could tell her that.
Dad had been dead exactly 2 years. Shiyo 790, April 8.
That day, Mom and I had gone to the Arata settlement, where the Niimura main family is, for Dad's third-year memorial service. [TN: Buddhist memorials count the year of death as the first year.]
There, I prayed to Dad with all my heart.
Please make those two disappear.
Dad was too close to be called an ancestor, but back then I didn't understand that.
No, curses aside, I was asking Dad for help.
That the bullying would stop, that Mom would get well, that we could go back to Motoki-cho soon.
The next day, word came that school was closed.
That was how I learned of the deaths of the two I had cursed. Someone had killed them, apparently.
I trembled with joy.
I never thought those two would really disappear. Curses... curses really exist...!
The police came to our home too, but naturally we weren't suspected.
Because Mom and I had an alibi: we'd been at the memorial service.
From around then, I started looking into curses in earnest.
Again, it was only children's-book-level stuff from the school library, but even so, the people around me found me creepy, and the bullying stopped just like that.
That's right, get rid of anyone who threatens my happiness or Mom's!
After looking into all sorts of things, I learned that the method from before was a way to summon a shinigami.
To summon the shinigami more reliably, instead of spitting on the paper doll, you smear it with vomit, in other words, what you've thrown up.
And you cast the curse on a day close to a relative's memorial service.
That way, on the day of the service, you can be sure to get rid of the person you want gone.
The next time I carried out a curse was right after I'd started sixth grade.
At 11, the kids around me could explain to their parents and teachers, in a fairly logical way, that I was "creepy."
I learned around this time that Megasawa City had once been a religious city dominated by a cult, and as a backlash, kids who played around with charms and curses were shunned.
The ones I cursed were the PTA president and my homeroom teacher.
Those two did whatever the kids and parents told them to, and bullied me in roundabout ways.
This time, it wasn't just kids. Even adults had joined in.
Being made out to be the one who stole the school lunch money was a given.
That I'd broken into a classmate's house to steal, that I'd smashed the windows of teachers' cars: accusations no 11-year-old could realistically have pulled off.
Each time, Mom went to the parents and teachers to apologize.
Mom must have known too, and yet her days were spent going around bowing her head over and over.
But Mom never once blamed me.
Far from it,
"If anything hurts, I'll help you, no matter what it is."
she would always tell me with a smile.
We didn't have the money to move, and Mom's strength was running out.
At this rate, Mom would end up back in the hospital.
Then life in Motoki-cho would slip further away again.
Before that happened, I had to get rid of the PTA president and my homeroom teacher.
Mom suffering because of me too... I couldn't stand it anymore.
Shinigami... lend me your power one more time...!
―On the day of Dad's seventh-year memorial service, it happened.
The results were beyond what I'd expected.
The PTA president had every tooth smashed with a blunt weapon, then was stabbed to death with a knife.
Not one stab hit anywhere that would kill instantly. The president must have suffered, waiting to bleed out, and died.
The homeroom teacher had all four limbs crushed with a blunt weapon and, unable to move, was killed by a toxic chemical poured into the mouth.
But once it had come this far, even I got scared.
It was the same as killing four people with my own hands.
This time too I explained the paper dolls to the police, but they didn't seem to take me seriously at all.
After that, the adults started avoiding me too.
Even though the bullying stopped, my mother and I were cut off from the kids and from the teachers.
Was this... what I had wanted...?
―Still holding that question, I went on to a public middle school in Megasawa City.
The middle school I went to was in a district that also took in graduates of another elementary school.
So a lot of them didn't know about my curses, and I started getting bullied again.
In middle school the methods were nastier, and even when they found out about my curses, many of them didn't believe it.
"Niimura, you're one of those cultists, I heard!?"
"Seriously gross! Cultists should just die."
I got hit with senseless abuse like that, but I wasn't going to curse anyone anymore.
Even someone like me had something that kept me going.
Natsumi's letters always cheered me up.
I didn't want to bring my everyday life into my one joy, so I never told Natsumi a thing about the bullying or the curses.

But those guys, even Natsumi...!
"What's this letter? You actually had a friend? Is this one a cultist too?"
"Give it back! That's my...!"
"I bet this one's a cultist too! 　You're gross, but this one's gross too."
"Okay, cult disbaaanded!"
"Let me have a go too!"
Insulting me was one thing, but Natsumi's letters getting torn up was something I just couldn't forgive.
Dad's tenth-year memorial service was 10 days away.
This is the last time.
Shinigami―Dad, those guys...!
One more time... just one more time...!
That night, I made the last paper doll.
And yet the next day, I was suddenly terrified.
No matter what, there was no need to kill them. There must have been some other curse.
No, first I had to lift this curse.
But on the morning of April 9, two bodies were found, more gruesome than ever before.
One had the arms and legs cut off and the belly slit open, the guts exposed, and crows were eating them.
The other had every bone in the body crushed and had been left in the mountains, where wild dogs had torn into it.
The shinigami no longer killed right away. It had begun making them suffer more before they died.
This time too the police didn't treat me as the culprit, but the school, the students, everyone came to fear me.
No, not just the people connected to the school. The residents nearby came to be afraid of me too.
And maybe it was because the municipality feared the old cult. In the end, a recommendation to change schools was issued to my mother and me.
Saying we should leave Megasawa City.
In the end, once you cast a curse, you can't stop.
I am a witch.
The witch of April 8, who summons the demon called the shinigami.
Whatever my reasons, I can only ever be something loathed.
As a result, we were able to go back to Motoki-cho, but the way it came about was nothing I would have wanted.
But there was nowhere else we could live.
The Arata settlement had some sort of rule, and apparently we weren't allowed to move there until I turned 16.
Though I'm already 17 now, life in Motoki-cho is stable, so there are no plans to move to the Arata settlement.
―Three years have passed since then.
Tomorrow is Shiyo 800, April 8. Dad's thirteenth-year memorial service.
But I asked Mom, and I'm skipping the thirteenth-year memorial service.
Because if I go to the service, a Megasawa City case will happen.
Mom agreed without any fuss. She must have understood how I felt.
Even without a paper doll, wouldn't a Megasawa City case happen anyway?
That fear has clung to me ever since I came to Motoki-cho.
I won't curse anyone anymore.
Since coming back to Motoki-cho, I've thrown away the witch I was, and I've made lots of friends.
Who knew just that much could let me live so brightly and happily.
Above all, by my side there's Natsumi, and my cute junior, Goto.
But I had one worry.
The day before yesterday, I got an upset stomach and threw up.
Maybe because food spoils easily once it gets warm, I'd come down with an upset stomach pretty much every year.
That was my only worry.
To cast a curse, I need my own vomit.
Except for the very first time, I'd gotten an upset stomach and vomited a few days before each curse.
No, it's fine. The shinigami won't appear this year.
There's no one I want to kill. The witch I was is gone now.
I opened the cabinet in the living room and looked at the memorial photo inside.
This is my dad, Eiichiro Niimura.
Dad died at 30.
He wasn't especially good-looking, and not even flattery could call his build average.
Because he went and forced himself up a tree with a body like that.
But Dad and Mom really were a close couple, and they doted on me.
"Dad, I'm sorry I can't come this year. I'm always thinking of you, Dad. My life here is a lot of fun. Natsumi and Goto and my other friends are all kind, and―"
Not having Dad here is very lonely.
Even so, I have never once felt hatred toward Natsumi.
Far from it, Natsumi is what keeps me going.
Natsumi is better off not knowing. Natsumi did nothing wrong.
That was... yes, that was... an accident...
