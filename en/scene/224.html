The Palm of the Hand

Shigatsu Arc [Death Month] · Shiyo 800-04-07 23:25 火 · 51 boxes

Niimura-senpai had gone out after her mother.
I couldn't leave Kogori-senpai and Kana-chan behind.
"Kana-chan, let's go sleep in a futon, okay? You'll get all sore sleeping here."
"Mm... nn... Eri-nee, don't leave me... Eri-nee, I wanna sleep with you... I'm scared..."
"Kana-chan... All right, then..."
Together with Kana-chan, I got into the futon next to the one Kogori-senpai was sleeping in.
Just as Niimura-senpai had held Kogori-senpai in her arms to get her to sleep, I tried to get Kana-chan to sleep, holding her in my arms.
―The whole time, I kept thinking.
This case really isn't a curse after all. There's human intent at work in it.
A lot of it looks like coincidence, but the culprit is acting with everything in view.
And not by running things on a single plan, either. The culprit has made plans for every pattern, so as to be ready for whatever happens.
Or else, a person sharp enough to respond at once to anything at all.
And a despicable person, who hems people in psychologically and has others carry out the plan without ever lifting a finger.
By this point, I had put together a certain hypothesis.
―Kotaro Niimura
Vague as it is, it's almost certain: this man is the culprit.
Just as Niimura-senpai said, people trust him, and he's smart.
That alone is more than enough to fit the picture of the culprit.
But Kotaro-san hasn't laid a hand on anyone, and there's probably no evidence, either.
On top of that, it's hard to believe he gave the actual killers any direct instructions.
Through extremely clever psychological steering, he's moving other people however he likes.
Yes, a method that's extremely clever, and despicable at the same time.
I hate this kind of criminal more than any other.
They do nothing themselves, treat other people like game pieces, make them kill each other, and get what they want.
If Kotaro-san is the culprit, I'll never forgive him...!
Whatever it takes, I'll catch him out and get him to the police...!
"Eri-nee..."
Kana-chan clung to me tightly.
"There, there. Nothing to be scared of..."
I gave Kana-chan's head a pat.
Kotaro-san has scared his own daughter this badly. What on earth is he trying to do...?
If Kotaro-san got caught, what would happen to Kana-chan?
Her mother is already dead, so Kana-chan would have no parents left.
Erika-chan, too: there's no telling how many more years she can live.
Then would Chiga-nee end up looking after Kana-chan?
After finally getting out to Tokyo, she'd be dragged back here by family circumstances.
However things fall, nothing changes the fact that Kana-chan will be hurt.
"Kana-chan..."
I hugged Kana-chan tight.
"Eri-nee...?"
Already half asleep, Kana-chan spoke in a small voice.
"Kana-chan, it's okay. You don't have to worry about anything..."
Kana-chan closed her eyes again.
How close can I get to the culprit?
Kotaro-san isn't proven to be the culprit, but even so, I'll build up my reasoning on that assumption, little by little.
This culprit who stays ahead of the other side's thinking at every turn, this cunning, despicable culprit... I'm going to reach them, no matter what!
What...?
No, it can't be...!
In that moment, something horrifying came into my head.
Could it be... that even my thinking this is part of the culprit's calculations?
If even this deduction is part of a plan the culprit foresaw, one step ahead of me...
Kotaro-san has been suspected of being the culprit by people in the past as well, it seems.
But however much they checked, no evidence turned up, and on top of that the curse-killings went on.
The people who suspected him were dancing to the culprit's tune in the end, too.
If so, no matter how much I build up my reasoning, I can never know where the culprit's expectations begin and end.
Am I in the palm of the culprit's hand right now?
If so, with my state of mind gripped this cleverly, I'll never reach the truth, however long I try...!
Or is even my noticing that I'm in the palm of the culprit's hand exactly what the culprit imagined?
This is going around in circles! However far I think it through, I don't feel like I'll ever get there...!
Then, a method the culprit could never think of...
Kotaro-san isn't settled as the culprit, but even so, it's enough to work with as a hypothesis.
Besides, just noticing that I may be in the culprit's hand is big progress.
I'll keep that firmly in mind as I build up my reasoning.
Kotaro-san is the culprit. Assuming that...
First, back to basics.
A crime always has a purpose.
9 years of curse-killings should be nothing more than a means.
Because the curse-killings are still going on.
That means either the purpose hasn't been achieved yet, or killing Kogori-senpai's parents was the purpose.
With the purpose this unclear, Kotaro-san must be hiding it in some clever way.
And in these curse-killings, the candidates for the 'setup' in kishotenketsu are far too many. [TN: Kishotenketsu: four parts, ki 'setup', sho 'development', ten 'twist', ketsu 'conclusion'.]
Not every strange thing that happened necessarily ties into the curse-killings.
So, first the purpose― it makes sense to start from the 'conclusion.'
Work the scenario out backward, and find the 'setup' from there.
That's the only method I have right now.
If I were Kotaro-san, what would I have done?
If we were getting close to the truth, I would definitely put in some misdirection.
For example, the kishotenketsu of the curse-killings.
A crime has a purpose, and only then do the means and the action, in other words the 'setup,' come about.
If I were the culprit, that's where I'd put in misdirection.
Thinking from the 'setup' only applies to mystery novels.
Because readers are only ever given the scenario in kishotenketsu order.
For the culprit, it always runs from the 'conclusion' to the 'setup.'
And the 'twist'― a situation that takes a sudden turn must never, ever happen.
The textbook way to commit a crime is, if possible, 'setup and conclusion' only, or at worst 'setup, development and conclusion' only.
In other words, thinking in kishotenketsu plays right into the culprit's hands.
Kishotenketsu is just how a story is built. For the culprit, the 'conclusion' is all that's needed.
So here I'll think only about the 'conclusion,' and think about the 'setup' once the 'conclusion' is settled.
For now, let me assume that killing Kogori-senpai's parents was the 'conclusion.'
Kogori-senpai's parents, then. If killing them is the purpose, what would the motive be?
One possibility: in connection with the Megasawa affair 10 years ago, Sakura-san died, and he resented Senpai's parents, who set it off.
But if that's why he wanted to kill them, the method is too roundabout.
In that case, all he'd have to do is get Kogori-senpai's parents to attend the thirteenth-year memorial service. [TN: Buddhist memorials count the year of death as the first year.]
Oh, the reason they didn't come to the thirteenth-year memorial service was Kogori-senpai's advice, wasn't it...?
If so, then ignoring Kogori-senpai's warning and coming near the settlement was a mistake after all.
If he noticed from some clue that Kogori-senpai's parents had come here...
Then, give some trigger to residents ruled by distrust, and what happened earlier would follow.
Right. As a theory, it fits, more or less.
Keeping the curse-killings going for as long as 9 years was a way to keep planting distrust in the residents, while psychologically steering Niimura-senpai's mother into asking Kogori-senpai's parents for help.
Committing the crime outside the settlement would surely be hard.
That's why Kotaro-san doesn't lay a hand on anyone himself, and the people around him commit the crime with nothing but a trigger from him―it took 9 years to bring things to that state.
But even so, that's too big a leap.
Even if the first curse-killing 9 years ago was done by Kotaro-san, or by a resident Kotaro-san talked into it, pulling it off without getting caught is a stretch.
It would be different if, say, he had the power to see the future...
Thinking from this supernatural angle goes against my principles.
Something like precognition...
Precognition...?
What am I saying...!
But there is someone...! Right near me, there's someone who has precognition...!
Kogori-senpai.
She has a precognition that works only at this time of year.
There's no saying she's the only person in the world with this ability.
From what I've heard...
Niimura-senpai's father died, and when he did, Kogori-senpai was drenched in his blood. After that, she awakened to that ability.
Kotaro-san, if he once had a chance to be drenched in blood the same way...
The causal link between the blood and the ability is unknown, but if this assumption is right, it explains everything...!
If so, there's no way an ordinary person could ever reach a truth like this! No wonder the incidents only happen in April!
If Kogori-senpai abused that ability, even something like this shouldn't be impossible.
In other words, as 'reckless intent,' it should be possible to set a trap so that the target of a curse-killing dies, or to egg someone on.
Because Kogori-senpai's ability is 'sensing danger to people.'
Use it in reverse, and you can put people in danger!
That much is my first hypothesis, the case where Kogori-senpai's parents were the final goal.
I need to test a different angle as well.
Besides... possible as it is, it's a bit too much of a leap.
The method is roundabout after all, and it needs the condition 'Kotaro-san has precognition.'
Then let's suppose killing Kogori-senpai's parents wasn't the purpose.
In that case, what on earth was the real purpose?
Suppose all the information I need is already here...
Kikyo-san's month of death, the many suicides, the curse-killings, the Megasawa affair, Sakura-san's death.
Come to think of it... I'd forgotten about that.
The drug that was supposedly almost used 10 years ago.
I can't see it being an important factor, but it's a special drug unique to Arata, so it might be worth starting from there.
Does everyone in Arata know the drug exists?
Sakura-san is said to have sent it, in which case Kotaro-san must know about it.
And Erika-chan must know about it as well.
To begin with, by what principle does this even act on the human body?
There's no way a magic drug that ignores every principle exists in this world.
By some principle or other, it must take the brain's limiter off.
Earlier, Senpai's father mentioned 'strength you only find in an emergency.'
If I remember right, the principle was that large amounts of adrenaline and noradrenaline are released, strongly activating the sympathetic nerves and dulling the muscle limiter.
A drug that makes that possible on the spot―should I think of it as a kind of so-called doping drug?
But if Sakura-san sent it to Niimura-senpai's mother, then far from stopping the murders, it would spur them on.
That's it... earlier, Kogori-senpai realized that and tried to ask Senpai's father, but he was killed right after.
Which means the culprit didn't want us to learn the details of the drug?
I knew it! Sakura-san didn't send the drug. The culprit must have secretly slipped it in with the supplies!
The curse-killings and the Megasawa City affair are connected!
On top of that, the culprit even sent a note on how to use the drug.
That means the culprit tried to get someone to kill people in Megasawa.
But I can't see the purpose of that.
To begin with, why did Sakura-san send weapons?
Erika-chan and Niimura-senpai's mother both testified to it, so there's little doubt that Sakura-san sent the weapons.
For example, maybe... she wanted the weapons and Noh masks turned into money to live on?
Niimura-senpai's mother is who I should have asked about that in more detail.
The ones targeted in Megasawa were two elementary school kids with no connection to either Sakura-san or Kotaro-san.
I can't imagine it was a personal grudge, and sympathy for Niimura-senpai's mother would be an obvious contradiction.
But still, even in this case, the odds that Kotaro-san is the culprit go up.
Because Niimura-senpai's mother went to Sakura-san about killing in Megasawa, this hypothesis doesn't hold unless Sakura-san let it slip to someone.
So the culprit is someone Sakura-san would likely tell―in other words, her husband, Kotaro-san.
Say, for example, the two of them had a conversation like this―
"Welcome back, Sakura. Why'd you go to Megasawa, of all places?"
"The truth is―"
"I see. So Mifuyu-chan wants the two elementary school kids..."
"Yes, but of course I stopped her. I can't do anything against the law or the rules."
"Yeah, I think that's right. Sakura, you made the right call."
If that's how it went, then getting Niimura-senpai's mother to commit murder was the purpose in itself.
If a murder happened in Megasawa, what would Kotaro-san even gain from it...?
This really is starting to scramble my head...
However far I go, I can't reach the culprit's purpose.
I want to hear from someone, but I can't leave Senpai and the others, and I don't think these two have any important information.
But... I want anything at all that gives me more to think with.
"Kana-chan, Kogori-senpai, please wake up."
I roused the two of them.
I don't like waking people by force, but in an emergency like this, it can't be helped.
Even truly trivial information would do.
I want information to think with.
