"Huh?"
"A curse..."
"A curse...?"

"That kind of curse?"

But if I deny this so-called curse here, it could turn into an unexpected loss of time.

But even so, I have to nod along and ask questions well, so I can draw out the information I need in the shortest time.

Then, Senpai let out a big breath.
"The first time I cursed someone to death was in a place called Megasawa City, where I lived 10 years ago... I just... couldn't bear it anymore."
"Megasawa City...?"
Megasawa City... Where the Megasawa City case happened, that one?

If I remember right, I had a memo about it somewhere...

"I see... That must have been hard."

Some kind of trouble had happened in Megasawa City.

No way... That can't be.

"In this incident too, Senpai, did you put a curse on Kogori-senpai and the others?"

"Probably, huh..."
What a vague answer.
If she'd flatly declared that she'd cast the curse, I could have asked, "Is there no way to lift it?" but...
No, if it could be lifted in the first place, Senpai wouldn't have come to me for advice.
In other words, there's no way to lift it.
"What do you mean by 'probably'?"
When I asked, Senpai took several sheets of paper out of the pocket of her sweats.
The crumpled sheets were being spread open by Senpai.
There were three sheets, with writing on them.
And, they were oddly shaped pieces of paper.
But, what this was, I― One look at that shape, and most people would probably have an idea.
"Paper dolls... are they?"
On the human-shaped paper were written the names of the three members of the Kogori family.
Ryoji Kogori, Senpai's father.
Akane Kogori, Senpai's mother.
And... Natsumi Kogori.
I see, so a person whose name is written on these paper dolls dies.
But, the odd stains all over them bother me.
Stains, or gunk, or whatever it was, they were pretty filthy.
"Senpai, so this is how you cursed someone to death 10 years ago."
"It's because I cursed..."
In a voice on the verge of tears again, Senpai said it.
"When I saw these this morning, I was really scared. While I was asleep, I did it without knowing, I'm sure..."
So the dolls were made sometime between last night and this morning.
That Senpai had these on her now was probably because she'd meant to show them to the police as evidence.
But, this paper-doll curse has one big contradiction.
Once she hears it, Senpai should realize this is a sham.
But, it'd probably be better not to let a smile show yet.
"But, Senpai? Kogori-senpai's father lied yesterday evening that he was going on a business trip, right? 　So at that point, the incident was already about to happen. In other words, if you think about the timing, doesn't that mean the paper dolls were added after the fact? 　This isn't a curse of yours, Senpai, or anything like it!"
"That's not it!!"
Suddenly Senpai shouted, and with a fearsome look, glared at me.
I was overwhelmed by Senpai's look and voice, and no words came out.
"Hahaha...! 　This stuff on the surface of the paper, you know what it is?"

"Huh? 　Um..."
I came back to myself and tried to answer Senpai's question, but her look cowed me and the words wouldn't come out right.
Not that I even knew what the stuff on the surface was in the first place.
"This is something I brought up."
Brought up?
What does she mean?
'Bring up' gets used to mean all sorts of things, but...
But, if it means exactly what it says―
"If you smear what you ate and brought up from your stomach onto a paper doll, the curse is complete. From then on, nothing you do can undo it."
A chill ran through me.
So in other words, vomit.
But while Senpai was asleep, she made paper dolls, wrote names on them, then brought something up from her stomach and smeared it on, and went back to bed as if nothing had happened, is what she's saying?
Something's off...! That's way too convenient!
In the first place, bringing it up on purpose, you might manage by sticking a finger down your throat, but then you'd surely wake up.

But...
"Natsumi's dad got in touch in the evening about a business trip? 　So what? He just happened to have some other errand, right? 　The curse just took hold while he was on that other errand. Come on, how can you say for sure that the business-trip lie leads to murder? 　Say it, then!"
